# Paper Notes: The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models
**arXiv: 2311.03658** | Park, Choe, Veitch (U Chicago) | ICML 2024

## Core Thesis
High-level concepts are represented linearly as directions in LLM representation spaces. The paper formalizes what "linear representation" means, identifies three interpretations (subspace, measurement, intervention), and unifies them via a "causal inner product."

## Three Notions of Linear Representation

1. **Subspace**: Concept = 1D subspace. E.g., Rep("queen") - Rep("king") is parallel to Rep("woman") - Rep("man").
2. **Measurement**: Concept can be detected with a linear probe. E.g., P(French) is logit-linear in context embedding.
3. **Intervention**: Adding a steering vector changes the concept value without affecting other concepts.

The paper proves these are all connected: unembedding representations connect to measurement (Thm 2.2), embedding representations connect to intervention (Thm 2.5).

## How They Define/Find Linear Directions for Concepts

### Concept formalization
- A **concept variable W** is a latent variable caused by context X that causes output Y.
- Binary concepts with an ordering (e.g., male=>female).
- **Counterfactual pairs**: pairs of outputs that differ only in concept value. E.g., (king, queen), (man, woman) for male=>female.
- **Causally separable** concepts: can vary freely and independently. E.g., English=>French and male=>female are separable.

### Unembedding Representation (Definition 2.1)
- gamma_bar_W is an unembedding representation if gamma(Y(1)) - gamma(Y(0)) lies in Cone(gamma_bar_W) almost surely.
- In other words, ALL counterfactual pairs point in the same direction in unembedding space.
- Estimated as the **normalized mean** of counterfactual pair differences:
  ```
  gamma_tilde_W = (1/n) * sum_i [gamma(y_i(1)) - gamma(y_i(0))]
  ```

### Embedding Representation (Definition 2.3)
- lambda_bar_W is an embedding representation if lambda_1 - lambda_0 lies in Cone(lambda_bar_W) for context pairs that differ only on the target concept.
- Hard to find directly; instead computed from unembedding representation via the causal inner product.

### Causal Inner Product (Key Innovation)
- An inner product where causally separable concepts are orthogonal: <gamma_bar_W, gamma_bar_Z>_C = 0
- **Practical form**: <gamma_bar, gamma_bar'>_C = gamma_bar^T * Cov(gamma)^{-1} * gamma_bar'
  - Where Cov(gamma) is the covariance of unembedding vectors over the vocabulary
- This choice (D = I_d) gives M = Cov(gamma)^{-1}
- Unifies embedding and unembedding representations via Riesz isomorphism (Thm 3.2)
- Euclidean inner product is NOT generally correct -- but sometimes approximately works (e.g., in LLaMA-2)

### Constructing Intervention (Steering) Vectors
- From unembedding representation: lambda_bar_W = Cov(gamma)^{-1} * gamma_bar_W
- Adding alpha * lambda_bar_W to context embedding changes target concept without affecting off-target concepts

## How They Validate/Probe

1. **Alignment test**: Project each counterfactual pair difference onto estimated concept direction using LOO. Counterfactual pairs should align much more than random pairs. Works for 26/27 concepts (exception: thing=>part).

2. **Orthogonality heatmap**: Causally separable concepts should be near-orthogonal under causal inner product. Figure 3 confirms this with block-diagonal structure matching semantic groupings.

3. **Linear probe test**: gamma_bar_W^T * lambda(x) separates French vs Spanish Wikipedia contexts (Figure 4). Off-target concept directions have no predictive power.

4. **Intervention test**: Adding alpha * lambda_bar_W linearly increases logit P(W=1|Z,lambda) while keeping P(Z=1|W,lambda) constant (Figure 5). E.g., adding male=>female direction makes "queen" more likely than "king" without changing "King" vs "king".

5. **Top-k word tracking**: After intervention, target word (e.g., "queen") rises to top-1 most probable completion (Table 1).

## Models Used
- **LLaMA-2-7B** (llama-2-7b variant): 7B params, 32K vocab, 4096-dim embeddings
- Also tested on **Gemma-2B** (in appendix) -- causal inner product works even better there since Euclidean fails

## Datasets/Concept Lists
- **27 concepts** total:
  - 22 from **BATS 3.0** (Big Analogy Test Set): verb conjugations, morphological transforms, semantic relations
  - 4 language pairs: English=>French, French=>German, French=>Spanish, German=>Spanish (using word translations as counterfactual pairs)
  - 1 frequency concept: frequent=>infrequent (synonym pairs like "bad"/"terrible")
- Context samples from Wikipedia pages in various languages
- Intervention contexts generated by ChatGPT-4
- Counterfactual pairs require single-token words in LLaMA-2 vocabulary

## Key Techniques Adaptable for "Is a Pokemon" Probing

1. **Counterfactual pair approach**: Could use (Pikachu, cat), (Charizard, dragon), etc. as counterfactual pairs for pokemon=>not_pokemon concept. Compute gamma("Pikachu") - gamma("cat") and check if these differences point in a common direction.

2. **Mean difference estimator**: Average the unembedding differences across all pokemon/non-pokemon pairs to get the concept direction.

3. **Causal inner product**: Use Cov(gamma)^{-1} to properly measure orthogonality and build intervention vectors. This is more principled than raw Euclidean cosine similarity.

4. **Intervention validation**: After finding a "pokemon direction," add it to context embeddings and check if model outputs shift toward pokemon-related completions.

5. **Probe as projection**: The concept direction gamma_bar_W itself acts as a linear probe -- just project context embeddings onto it.

## Important Caveats
- Works best with concepts that can be cleanly expressed as single-token counterfactual pairs
- The paper focuses on the unembedding (output) space, not intermediate residual stream layers
- The causal inner product formulation requires access to the model's unembedding matrix
- Not all concepts have linear representations (thing=>part failed)
- The paper does not address intermediate layer activations or mechanistic interpretability of model internals
